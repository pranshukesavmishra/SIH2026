01

How SIH is actually won

The mechanics decide the strategy, and most teams never do this arithmetic.

SIH does not have one national winner. Every problem statement has its own winner, with its own prize — ₹1 lakh through SIH 2023 and 2024, raised to ₹1.5 lakh in 2025. Your competition is never "all of India". It is the handful of teams assigned to your statement at your nodal centre.

The funnel runs: college internal hackathon → your SPOC nominates up to 30 teams (25 shortlisted, 5 waitlisted) → idea submission on the portal, capped at 500 per statement → roughly 5 to 8 teams per statement reach the Grand Finale. At IIT Roorkee's 2025 hardware finale, 23 teams competed across 5 problem statements — under 5 teams per statement.

So your odds of being shortlisted are approximately 6 ÷ (submissions on your PS). That single number swings by more than an order of magnitude depending on nothing but which statement you type into the form.

02

What five editions of winners have in common

SIH 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 — the 2026 edition is the ninth; there was no SIH 2021.

  1. Completeness beats ambition.The consistent judging guidance across editions: a stable working prototype that fully answers the stated problem outscores a larger vision with holes in it. Scoring runs across multiple rounds, 1–20 per criterion, weighted to 100, with a final "power round" that decides close calls.
  2. Deep-domain defence entries win with real engineering.SIH 2023's Ministry of Defence statement was won by IIT Bombay's Team Avlokan with a quadplane VTOL carrying a boom-deployed magnetic anomaly detection suite for submarine detection — not an app, an instrument. Hard statements are won by teams that treat them as engineering, and those teams are rare.
  3. The organisations adopt what they can use.After SIH 2024, ISRO accepted four solutions for in-house development (solar panel tilt adjustment, video geotagging, cyclone intensity estimation from INSAT-3D IR imagery, and blockchain storage for large geospatial datasets); DRDO accepted one (gesture-enabled PC commands). Build to the sponsor's operational spec, not to a demo.
  4. Solve the stated problem, not a buzzword adjacent to it.Judges reward how effectively you answer the brief, not how many technologies you stack. Feasibility, cost-effectiveness, scalability and social relevance recur in every edition's criteria.
  5. Nothing about the winners is glamorous.SIH 2025's software national winner — team Passengers, IUST Awantipora — built an API bridging AYUSH NAMASTE to ICD-11 for EMR compliance. A standards-mapping API. Unfashionable, precisely scoped, completely delivered.
03

The crowd-repellence index

Live submission counts were not reachable from this environment, so crowding is modelled structurally from each statement's own text.

Every statement's title and full description was scored for two opposing vocabularies. One attracts volume — portal, dashboard, chatbot, mobile app, gamified, recommendation. The other repels it — SAR, interleaving, Viterbi, ephemeris, IPsec, photoclinometry, dead reckoning, electronic warfare. Combined with acronym density and description length, this gives a proxy for how many student teams will actually attempt the statement.

The distinction matters because the low-count strategy is widely known — the submission counter is public on the portal, and guides have told students to sort by it for years. Statements that are merely unnoticed get swarmed in the final week. Statements that are low-count for structural reasons — a skill barrier the crowd cannot cross in three weeks — stay low. Repellence is the durable moat; obscurity is not.

05

Building SIH26169 end to end

Ten modules. Every one of them is code you write; none of them waits on anyone else.

The statement asks for a software system that generates a configurable virtual environment and moving beacons, implements a movable virtual camera, detects and continuously tracks the beacon with computer vision, repositions the camera, injects atmospheric turbulence and platform vibration, and reports tracking statistics in real time. Mandatory deliverables are a standalone executable, documented modular source, a 10–15 page technical report, a user manual, and an automatic performance log covering simulation duration, FPS, acquisition time, average and maximum tracking error, lock retention rate and processing time.

M1Scene generator

Configurable virtual world — sky gradients, star fields, terrain, cloud and clutter layers, moving distractors. Offscreen rendering, seeded and reproducible.

M2Beacon model

One or more targets on scripted trajectories (LEO pass, UAV waypoints, random walk) with a real radiometric model: irradiance falloff, Airy-disc PSF, modulated blink pattern.

M3Virtual pan-tilt camera

FOV, focal length, pixel pitch, resolution, frame rate, exposure, ADC quantisation, gimbal rate and acceleration limits, and command latency.

M4Disturbance engine

Kolmogorov phase screens for turbulence-induced tip/tilt and scintillation, PSD-driven platform vibration, shot/read/dark noise, sun glint, dropped frames.

M5Detection

Two independent paths: matched-filter/centroid classical detection, and a small CNN trained on your own synthetic frames. Each is a fallback for the other.

M6Tracking

Kalman or IMM filter with data association, explicit lock/loss state machine, and a spiral re-acquisition search after loss. Handles multiple beacons and decoys.

M7Pointing control

Closed-loop PID with velocity feed-forward from the track, rate limiting, and latency compensation so the loop stays stable at realistic gimbal delays.

M8Metrics engine

Every logged quantity the statement names, in pixels and microradians, plus detection probability versus SNR curves. Auto-exports the performance report.

M9Operator GUI

Live camera view with track overlay, full parameter panel, real-time error plots, scenario load/save as YAML, one-click report export.

M10Packaging

Single standalone executable, commented modular source, technical report, user manual, and the optional 3–5 minute demonstration video.

Going beyond the brief

The statement is the floor. These are what separate a delivered project from a winning one, and each one is defensible as directly serving ISRO's stated purpose — a cheap platform for developing PAT algorithms.

  • Close the PAT chain. Model a quadrant photodiode and fine steering mirror and demonstrate coarse → fine handoff. The brief stops at coarse; you finish the story.
  • Hardware in the loop. A webcam, a two-axis servo pan-tilt and an LED beacon costs under ₹3,000 and proves the identical code drives real optics. The brief's own premise is that hardware is prohibitively expensive — showing the transfer is the single strongest thing you can put in front of an ISRO judge.
  • Real orbital geometry. Drive trajectories from actual TLEs via Skyfield so your angular rates match a genuine ground-to-LEO pass instead of an invented one.
  • Link budget coupling. Convert pointing error into FSO link availability and BER, so tracking accuracy maps to communications performance rather than pixels.
  • Monte Carlo campaign. A thousand randomised scenarios producing a statistical performance envelope, not a single lucky demo run.
  • Baseline comparison. Benchmark against OpenCV's KCF and CSRT trackers and plain centroiding, with the results table in the report.

Schedule, from today

  1. Now → 3 SepLock the statement. Assign the six roles. Read the FSOC PAT literature. Build a minimal renderer with a moving dot and a centroid tracker — you need a screenshot and a number for the proposal.
  2. 4 → 10 SepInternal college hackathon. Prepare the idea submission around preliminary results, not slides. Almost nobody submits measured performance at this stage; it is the cheapest differentiation available.
  3. By 20 SepIdea submission on the portal. Verify the deadline yourself — the portal snapshot says 20 September, some secondary sources say 30 September.
  4. OctoberFull simulator, disturbance engine, tracker v2, control loop, metrics engine. Target: the complete mandatory scope working, unpolished.
  5. NovemberGUI, packaging, hardware-in-the-loop rig, Monte Carlo campaign, technical report and user manual. Freeze features by end of month.
  6. DecemberRehearse the demo until it cannot fail. Keep two or three planned extensions in your pocket to implement live during the 36 hours — judges score visible progress across rounds.

Six roles for six people

Simulation and rendering · computer vision and detection · estimation, control and metrics · GUI, packaging and scenario tooling · hardware-in-the-loop rig and demo operation · report, manual and presentation. The last role is not a consolation prize — the technical report and user manual are named deliverables, and most teams treat them as an afterthought the night before.

07

What to verify before you commit

Limits of this analysis, stated plainly.

Live submission counts could not be retrieved

sih.gov.in and every public mirror are blocked by this environment's network policy. Both dataset snapshots available were taken on 21–22 August, when every statement still read 0/500. The crowding analysis here is modelled from statement text, not measured. Open the portal yourself, sort the defence cluster by submitted count around 10–14 September, and confirm that 26169, 26055 and 26168 are genuinely light before you lock in. If one of them has unexpectedly swarmed, move to SIH26159 or SIH26147.

Three data caveats

Difficulty levels and scores quoted anywhere in this dossier are community-derived estimates from a public dataset repository, not official SIH ratings. One of the two mirrors had its theme column misaligned against every row — themes here come from the mirror that cross-checks correctly. And the idea submission deadline reads 20 September on the portal snapshot while some secondary write-ups say 30 September; treat 20 September as the real one until your SPOC confirms otherwise.

Hedge with your college's other teams

Your SPOC nominates up to 30 teams. Coordinate so your college covers 26169, 26055 and 26159 across different teams rather than three teams piling onto the same statement — you multiply the college's shortlisting surface without diluting any single submission, and none of the three overlaps in required skills.
